## A joke that grew into a library

Years ago I started a small online community called **只买书不读书** — "only buying books, never reading them." Everyone who joined understood the affliction: the shelves keep growing taller, but the stack you've actually finished stays short. Buying books is a kind of illness; the satisfaction at checkout so often outlasts the patience to open them.

That self-mocking name stayed with me. When I finally decided to move my hoard back to my hometown and turn it into a free village library, I kept the joke and called it **只买书 (Zhimaishu)** — "Only Buying Books." Half in jest, half in earnest: if one person can't read them all, let more people come and read. My book-buying habit finally has something to show for itself.

## An old house, two walls of books

The library has no building of its own. It lives inside my family's thirty-year-old red brick house — the place where I grew up, in the middle of my home village. Two walls of the main room are lined, floor to nearly ceiling, with wooden shelves.

These are books I bought one at a time over decades — about fifty cardboard boxes, shipped all the way back from Shanghai. For days the house smelled of paper and old bindings. They once belonged to a city apartment; now they've come home to a country house, waiting for new readers.

## Like the treasure of Monte Cristo

Why do this?

As a child, I stumbled on a pile of picture books and a copy of *The Wizard of Oz* in my grandfather's old house. It felt like the Count of Monte Cristo unearthing a centuries-old treasure on a lonely island — I fell headlong into stories and never quite climbed back out. Those books are why I love reading.

I often think: if that old house hadn't held those few books, I'd be a different person. So this library is really a treasure left waiting to be found. I've placed it here as a wager — that some book-loving village child will push open the door, just as I once did, and meet the book that changes their life.

## Free to read, borrow on your honor

The library is unstaffed and costs nothing. No library card, no librarian, no shutter that comes down at closing time. Come in whenever you like, sit and read, or take a book home and return it when you're done.

What holds it all together is something old and precious: **your conscience**. Please treat the books as you would your own — return them to the shelf when you finish, so the next person can find them too.
